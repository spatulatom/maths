Putting it together

Eighteen lessons boil down to a short list of questions a good analyst asks of every number. Here's the list, and a mixed quiz to prove it's stuck.

⏱ 30–40 minutes, works on a phone ✎ Mixed: a little arithmetic, mostly judgement 🎯 Win: 12 questions across every stage, and a checklist to keep

The analyst's checklist

Keep this next to you during the Coursera course and your first projects. Each row is a stage, the question it trains you to ask, and where to look it up again.

StageAsk yourselfLessons
PercentagesPercent of what base? Percent change or percentage points?0001
AveragesMean or median? Is this an average of averages that needs weighting?0002, 0003
GrowthCompared to when: last month or last year? Is it smoothed? Over several years, CAGR?0004–0006
Spread & shapeHow spread out? Skewed? Which outliers did I check, and how?0007–0009
RelationshipsDid I look at the plot? Correlation, or proven cause? Am I extrapolating?0010, 0011
UncertaintyWho's in the sample? What's the margin? Significant and big enough to matter?0012–0015
LogicWhich rows survived the join and the filters? Did the row count change? AND or OR?0016–0018

Three habits that tie it together

  1. Look before you summarise. Plot it first: a histogram before an average, a scatter before r, a row count before a total.
  2. Always say "compared to what". A number alone means little. Last year, the target, the other store, or the margin of error give it meaning.
  3. Pick words that match the evidence. "Is associated with" for correlations, "about £42, give or take £5" for samples, "drives" only after an experiment.
