# A/B tests and p-values mastered

Lesson 0015 quiz scored 8/8. The learner can read a p-value correctly: if there were no real difference, how often luck alone would give a gap this big. They know that "significant" doesn't mean "big", "not significant" doesn't mean "the same", and that running many tests or peeking every day creates false wins.

The questions covered:

- why a random split shows cause (it balances confounders)
- what p = 0.02 means (not "a 2% chance B is better")
- p = 0.30: luck could explain it; not proof the versions are identical
- huge sample, tiny gap, p = 0.001: likely real but may be too small to matter
- 100 visitors and no significant result: the test was too small to tell
- 20 metrics, one with p = 0.04: about one in 20 is expected by luck
- peeking daily and stopping at p < 0.05 inflates false positives
- spaced review: margin of error for an average (SD 40, n 400 → SE 2 → ± £4)
