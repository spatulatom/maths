Reference · Math for Data Analysis

Glossary

The words this course uses, and only terms you've already shown you can use.

Each lesson sticks to these words. Terms are added once a quiz shows you've got them, not before.

Percentages

Percent change
How much a value moved, relative to where it started: (new − old) ÷ old.
Avoid: "growth" when the direction is unclear; never divide by the new value.
Percentage point
The plain difference between two percentages: 4% → 5% is +1 percentage point (and a +25% percent change).
Avoid: "went up 1%" when you mean one percentage point.

Averages

Mean
The sum of the values divided by how many there are. Excel AVERAGE.
Avoid: plain "average" in this course. Say mean, median, or weighted average, since "average" alone is ambiguous.
Why the odd name: from Latin medianus, "middle". It has nothing to do with "mean" as in unkind.
Median
The middle value once the values are sorted. With an even count, it's the mean of the two middle values. Excel and DAX MEDIAN.
Outlier
A value an abnormal distance from the rest of the data. A common flag is more than 1.5 × IQR beyond the middle half (taught in stage 5), but judgement decides.
Avoid: "error" or "bad data" until you know why it's there.
Skewed data
Data where most values bunch together and a few stretch far out to one side, pulling the mean away from the median.
Avoid: "lopsided" in reports (fine for intuition).
Weighted mean
A mean where each value counts in proportion to its weight: sum of (weight × value) ÷ sum of weights. The value is the thing being averaged (its unit is the answer's unit); the weight says how many or how much. Excel SUMPRODUCT(weights, values) / SUM(weights).
Avoid: averaging averages directly. Turn averages back into totals, add the totals, then divide.

Growth over time

MoM (month over month)
Percent change from the previous month: (this month − last month) ÷ last month.
Avoid: reading a MoM drop as bad news in seasonal data without checking YoY.
YoY (year over year)
Percent change from the same period one year earlier. It compares like with like, so the season cancels out.
Seasonality
A pattern that repeats at the same time every year (ice cream peaks in summer, retail in December).
Avoid: "trend" for a seasonal swing. A trend is the longer-run direction underneath.
