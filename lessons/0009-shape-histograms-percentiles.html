Lesson 0009 · Stage 5: Spread & shape

What shape is it?

Histograms, skewed data, and percentiles: look at the shape first, then pick the right summary.

⏱ 20–25 minutes, works on a phone ✎ Mostly reasoning, a little counting 🎯 Win: read a histogram, name its shape, and say which average and spread to report
New words
Distribution
How the values are spread across their range: where they pile up and where they thin out. Its "shape".
Histogram
A bar chart of a distribution. Each bar counts how many values fall in one range.
Bin
One of those ranges, for example €20 to €40. All bins have the same width.
Skewed
Lopsided. Right-skewed means a long tail of big values on the right. Left-skewed means a long tail on the left.
Percentile
The value with a given percentage of the data at or below it. P90 has 90% of values at or below it.

From a list to a picture

A dot plot works for 10 values, but not for 10,000. A histogram groups values into equal-width bins and draws one bar per bin showing how many values landed there.

Example: 30 order values with bins €0–20, €20–40, €40–60 and so on. If 15 orders fall between €20 and €40, that bar is 15 high. Tools differ on where a value sitting exactly on an edge goes. The chart below puts €40 in €40–60, but other tools may put it in the bin below, so don't be surprised if the counts shift by one.

Three shapes to recognise

ShapeLooks likeMean vs medianTypical business data
SymmetricOne hump in the middle, even on both sides (bell-shaped)About equalHeights, measurement errors, test scores
Right-skewedHump on the left, long tail to the rightMean above medianOrder values, incomes, house prices, delivery times, website session lengths
Left-skewedLong tail to the left, hump on the rightMean below medianScores on an easy exam, product ratings out of 5

Why does the mean move? It gets pulled toward the tail, because a few huge values add a lot to the total. The median only cares about the middle position, so it stays put (OpenIntro Statistics, §2.1). This is the same effect you saw with one outlier in Lesson 0002, spread across a whole tail.

Most business money data is right-skewed

Amounts can't go below zero, but a few customers, deals or houses can be enormous. That's why "average salary" usually sounds higher than what a typical person earns. When you see money, expect a right skew and check the median.

Percentiles: the general version of quartiles

You've already met three percentiles without the name: Q1 = P25, median = P50, Q3 = P75. Any other percentage works the same way.

Businesses love P90 and P95 because they describe the bad-but-normal case. "Median delivery is 3 days" sounds great. But "P95 delivery is 9 days" tells you that 1 order in 20 takes 9 days or longer, and those are the customers who complain.

In Excel: =PERCENTILE.INC(B2:B500, 0.9). The second argument is a fraction from 0 to 1, so 0.9 means P90 (Microsoft Support).

Try it

Thirty order values (example figures, €). The solid line is the median, the dashed line is the mean, and the green dotted line is the percentile you choose.

Experiments

  1. Change the bin width to 5, then to 50. Same data, but does the shape look the same? Bin width is a choice, and a bad one can hide the shape.
  2. Delete the 120 and 160. Which line moves more, the mean or the median?
  3. Set the percentile to 50. What do you notice? Then try 25 and 75. (They may differ slightly from your hand-calculated quartiles, for the same reason as in Lesson 0007.)
  4. Type in something symmetric, like 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, with bin width 1. Where do the mean and median sit?

Where you'll meet this

In Excel: Insert → Insert Statistic Chart → Histogram. Excel picks a bin width automatically, and you can change it under Format Axis (Microsoft Support: Create a histogram).

In Power BI: there's no one-click histogram. Instead you create bins on a numeric column (right-click the field → New group, set the bin size) and then put the bins on a column chart (Microsoft Learn: Grouping and binning). "Use grouping, binning, and clustering" is a listed PL-300 skill (study guide). DAX preview, nothing to memorise: PERCENTILE.INC(Orders[Value], 0.9).

In interviews: "Our average order value went up 20%. Is that good news?" A strong answer: "Maybe. Order values are usually right-skewed, so a few huge orders could have moved the mean while the typical order didn't change. I'd check the median and the histogram."
