Lesson 0004 · Stage 4: Growth over time

Compared to when?

Month over month vs year over year, and why the wrong one can start a panic.

⏱ 15–20 minutes, works on a phone ✎ Mental math is enough 🎯 Win: pick MoM or YoY for a question and calculate it
New words
Period
One slice of time in a report: a day, month, quarter, or year.
Time series
The same number measured period after period, like monthly sales.
MoM
Month over month: this month compared with the month just before.
YoY
Year over year: this period compared with the same period one year earlier.
Seasonality
A pattern that repeats every year: ice cream peaks in summer, toy shops in December.
Time intelligence
Power BI's name for calculations like MoM and YoY. It's on the PL-300 skills list.

You already know the formula

MoM and YoY are both just percent change, which you got right every time so far:

(new − old) ÷ old

The only new question is which old value to use. That choice changes the story completely.

"Old" value is…Answers the question…
MoMlast month"What changed just now?"
YoYthe same month last year"Are we doing better than a year ago?"

See it on real-shaped data

Below are monthly sales for a small ice cream shop (example figures, in thousands of euros). September 2026 is selected. Switch between MoM and YoY, then tap other months.

In MoM mode, September looks like a disaster: −33%. In YoY mode it's +12%, a healthy year. Same shop, same month, opposite stories.

The MoM drop is just seasonality: September is always cooler than August. YoY compares September with September, so the season cancels out. Repeating patterns like this are one of the basic shapes analysts look for in any time series (Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice).

Rule of thumb

If the business has seasons, judge performance with YoY. Use MoM to spot sudden changes, like a website outage or a price change, and always check it against last year before you raise an alarm.

Where you'll meet this

In Excel: with months in rows, MoM in row 3 is =(B3-B2)/B2. YoY with 12 rows per year is =(B14-B2)/B2, pointing 12 rows up.

In Power BI (preview, nothing to memorise): DAX has ready-made time functions. SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR means "the same dates, one year back" (DAX docs). A YoY measure is your percent-change formula with that function supplying the "old" value. The PL-300 study guide lists "Implement time intelligence measures" as an exam skill (study guide).

The first period is always blank

The first month in the data has no previous month, and the first year has no "last year". The honest answer is blank, not 0% and not 100%. DAX's DIVIDE returns blank here for exactly this reason.
