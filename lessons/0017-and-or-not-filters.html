Lesson 0017 · Logic

AND, OR, NOT

Every filter, slicer, and COUNTIFS is a small logic sentence. Get the sentence right and the numbers follow.

⏱ 20–25 minutes, works on a phone ✎ No calculation, all logic 🎯 Win: predict how Power BI combines slicers, and avoid the NOT-and-blanks trap
New words
Condition
A yes/no test on a row: "Amount > 100" is true or false for each row.
AND
Keep the row only if all conditions are true. Narrows.
OR
Keep the row if at least one condition is true. Widens.
NOT
Flip a condition: true becomes false, false becomes true.
Boolean
A true/false value. Named after George Boole, who wrote down these rules.

Adding a condition with AND can only keep the same rows or fewer. Adding one with OR can only keep the same or more. If a filtered number looks too big or too small, check which word joins the conditions.

The classic slip

A manager asks for "sales in North and South". In logic, that's Region = North OR Region = South. Written as "Region = North AND Region = South", it returns nothing: no single row can be in two regions.

Brackets change the meaning

  • (North OR South) AND Amount > 100: big orders from either region.
  • North OR (South AND Amount > 100): all North orders, plus only the big South ones.

Same words, different rows. When in doubt, add brackets, just like with order of operations in stage 1.

NOT, and the "neither" rule

NOT (North OR South) means "neither North nor South", which equals NOT North AND NOT South. The NOT flips the OR into an AND. (This is called De Morgan's law, and it explains many "why is my filter wrong?" moments.)

Watch out: blanks

"Returned = Yes" is false for a row where Returned is blank. So NOT (Returned = Yes) keeps the blank rows too, along with the genuine "No" rows. If you meant "confirmed not returned", filter on Returned = No instead.

Try it

Ten sales rows. Tick conditions, flip them with NOT, and choose AND or OR. Green rows are kept.

Experiments

  1. It starts with Region = North AND Amount > 100. Switch to OR. How many more rows appear?
  2. Untick everything. Tick Region in (North, South) and Amount > 100 with AND. That's "(North OR South) AND big".
  3. Untick everything. Tick Returned = Yes and press its NOT. Which row sneaks in that isn't a "No"?
  4. Tick Region = North and Product = Tea. Compare the totals with AND and with OR.

Where you'll meet this

In Excel: =AND(B2="North", C2>100), =OR(…) and =NOT(…) return TRUE or FALSE. COUNTIFS and SUMIFS combine their conditions with AND. In the FILTER function, * between conditions means AND and + means OR.

In Power BI, the rule worth memorising:

  • Several values in one slicer or filter (North and South ticked): combined with OR.
  • Filters on different fields (Region slicer + Product slicer): combined with AND.

DAX preview, don't learn it yet: CALCULATE([Sales], Sales[Region] = "North", Sales[Amount] > 100). In plain words: sales where the region is North AND the amount is over 100. Filters listed with commas in CALCULATE combine with AND; for OR inside a filter, DAX uses ||.

In interviews: "A dashboard shows £0 when the user picks North and South in two different slicers on the same Region column. Why?" A strong answer: "Two slicers on the same field combine with AND, and no row is both North and South. Use one slicer with both values ticked, which combines them with OR."
